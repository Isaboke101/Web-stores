How Much Does a Website Cost in Kenya in 2026?

By Isaac Oonge · Founder, Kūgeria Ltd · July 2026 · 5 min read

"How much for a website?" is the first question every Kenyan business owner asks — and the most common answer they get is a frustrating "it depends." That answer is technically true, but it's not helpful. So here is a transparent breakdown of what websites actually cost in Kenya in 2026, what drives the price, and how to avoid overpaying.

The Short Answer: Typical Price Ranges

These ranges reflect custom-built work by professional developers in Kenya. You will find cheaper offers — and that gap is worth understanding before you choose.

What Actually Drives the Price

Five factors account for almost all of the variation:

  • Number of pages and custom designs. Every unique page layout is design plus development time.
  • Functionality. A contact form is simple. Payment integration, booking systems, user accounts, and dashboards each add real engineering work.
  • Payment integration. M-Pesa (Daraja API) integration done properly — with confirmation callbacks and error handling — is skilled work, not a plugin.
  • Content and branding. If you need copywriting, a logo, or photography, that's additional scope.
  • Who builds it. A template flipped in an afternoon costs less than software engineered for your business — and performs like it.

The Hidden Costs Nobody Mentions

Your quote is not the full picture. Budget for these recurring items:

  • Domain name: roughly KES 1,000–1,500/year for a .co.ke
  • Hosting: KES 3,000–10,000/year depending on the provider and traffic
  • SSL certificate: often free (Let's Encrypt), but confirm it's included
  • Maintenance: optional, typically KES 2,000–10,000/month for updates, backups, and small changes
Red flag: if a developer quotes a very low price but you don't own the code, the domain, or the hosting account afterwards — you're renting, not buying. Always confirm in writing that you own your website outright.

Template vs Custom: The Real Trade-Off

Template sites (WordPress themes, page builders) are cheaper upfront and fine for basic needs. But they come with weight: slower load times, generic layouts your customers have seen elsewhere, plugin dependencies that break, and limits the moment you need something the template didn't anticipate.

Custom-built sites cost more upfront but load faster, rank better on Google, look like your brand rather than a theme, and grow with your business instead of against it. For a business whose website is meant to generate revenue, custom work usually pays for itself.

How to Get an Accurate Quote

Before approaching any developer, prepare three things: what you want the website to do (not just look like), examples of 2–3 sites you admire, and an honest budget range. A good developer will then give you a fixed, itemised quote — and explain exactly what each shilling buys.
